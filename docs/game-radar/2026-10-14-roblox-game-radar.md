## What players want right now (convergent across all 6 scouts)

1. **The steal/hatch/collector loop is still the single biggest demand driver by raw
   CCU** — Steal an Egg hit ~1.1M CCU and 300M+ plays before Roblox restricted it,
   Steal a Brainrot set the platform's all-time CCU record (25.8M peak), and Grow a
   Garden peaked at 22.3M. Demand provably *survived* moderation — a copycat "Steal An
   Egg ROLEPLAY" is still in Rotrends' Oct 14 top-10 — but the lane is saturated,
   litigated (Steal a Brainrot's owners are suing 4+ copycat studios), and Roblox has
   now banned the "continuous feed" treadmill mechanic specifically. **Verdict: the
   steal-and-bank core loop is still compliant and provable — the brainrot skin and the
   feed mechanic are what to drop.**
2. **Horror-escape is the sharpest, most-evidenced whitespace this week** — the
   buildability scout quantifies it directly: only ~78K CCU spread across 5 tracked
   horror-escape titles vs ~763.6K CCU across all 15 escape-genre games (high demand,
   thin supply, concentrated in 3–4 incumbents). Reddit's "Recommend Me A Game" threads
   are actively asking for options beyond Doors/Forsaken/Flee the Facility, and
   YouTube/Google both show live Halloween-driven momentum (Forsaken, CHAIN [HALLOWS],
   SCP Morph trending; platform CCU hit 15.1M, +31% WoW) ahead of Roblox's official
   "The Hollow" event (~Oct 22). It's also the best architectural match for this dev's
   stack — a near-direct reuse of the existing maze/procedural-gen engine and DataStore
   chapter-save pattern.
3. **Tower defense (anime/gacha-flavored) is a durable, non-spike signal** — Anime
   Vanguards, Alliance TD, and Multiverse TD are all growing simultaneously rather than
   one winner-take-all hit, showing room for a tightly-scoped single-map entry — but it
   needs real combat systems/netcode and content-refresh cadence that's a weaker fit
   for a solo dev's current toolkit than the other picks.
4. **Puzzle and digging/mining sims show an execution-quality gap, not a demand gap** —
   Puzzle is rated "Low" competition / 0.68 opportunity score (one of the least
   saturated tracked genres) and maps almost directly onto the existing maze-obby
   engine. Meanwhile r/RobloxGameDev's top "Looking For Devs" flair this week (31
   posts) centers on solo-built digging games, but DevForum feedback on multiple live
   mining incrementals repeatedly flags the *same* unsolved problems: repetitive
   auto-dig loops, no skill-based interaction, confusing controls, cluttered inventory
   UI, and lag — a rare "fix the obvious bugs everyone already complains about"
   opening rather than a new genre bet.
5. **Round-based, no-combat physics survival is an inferred (not directly evidenced)
   whitespace** — TikTok shows short, highly-clippable "moment" formats (Admin Abuse,
   keyboard-ASMR speed obbies) dominating current tags because simplicity of the moment
   beats depth, while the buildability scout flags PvP/fighting netcode as the hardest
   genre for a solo dev to support. A round-based, physics-destruction arena with no
   hit-reg threads that needle, but no scout names a current breakout exemplar, so it's
   ranked lower on confidence.

Cross-cutting edge: **this dev's maze/procedural-gen engine and DataStore
chapter/level-save pattern port almost directly into both horror-escape (per-chapter
checkpoints) and puzzle-escape (per-level completion state)** — the two top-ranked
opportunities this week are also the two cheapest to build from existing code.

**Avoid solo:** 1:1 brainrot-skin / continuous-feed clones (banned mechanic + active
litigation), anime-licensed tower defense requiring real combat systems and constant
content refreshes, competitive PvP/fighting genres (Blade Ball, Strongest Battlegrounds
— netcode and hit-reg are explicitly the hardest fit for a solo dev's stack).

## Concept briefs (top 4)

### 1. Undersink: Horror Escape · score 8.35

- **Hook:** The facility doesn't have a map — because it rewrites its own corridors
  every run, so you survive by reading the room, not memorizing it.
- **Core loop:** Drop into a short "chapter" (5–10 min) inside Undersink, a flooded
  underground research facility. Each run, the maze-gen engine reshuffles corridor
  layout, hazard placement, and where the stalking entity spawns, so players read
  environmental tells (flickering lights, water flow, vent noise, blood/sludge trails)
  instead of recalling a fixed route. Explore, solve 1–2 light environmental
  puzzles/keycard pulls, dodge or outlast a hunting entity, and reach the chapter's
  extraction point before a facility-collapse timer runs out. Success saves via
  per-chapter DataStore checkpoints — a death only costs the current chapter, not the
  whole run.
- **Why it trends:** Three signals point the same way: horror-escape sits at only ~78K
  CCU across 5 tracked titles vs ~763.6K CCU across all 15 escape-genre games (proven
  demand, thin supply, concentrated in 3–4 incumbents); Reddit is actively asking for
  asymmetric/escape-horror beyond Doors/Forsaken/Flee the Facility; and horror
  (Forsaken, CHAIN [HALLOWS], SCP Morph) is occupying top trending slots during a
  platform-wide Halloween CCU surge to 15.1M (+31% WoW), just before Roblox's official
  "The Hollow" event. A procedurally-regenerating facility is the original twist that
  avoids a 1:1 Doors clone.
- **Solo build scope:** 1–3 week MVP. Repurpose the existing maze/procedural-gen
  engine to shuffle a small library of modular corridor/room pieces (8–12 to start)
  plus randomized hazard/entity spawn tables per chapter. Ship 3 chapters at launch
  (reskins of the same piece library, escalating hazard density), one extraction point
  + collapse-timer each. One hunting entity with a simple patrol → investigate →
  chase state machine using Roblox's built-in pathfinding. Reuse the existing DataStore
  chapter/level-save pattern verbatim for checkpoints + cosmetic/lore-note unlocks.
  Single server, no matchmaking/teams for v1. Keep art bar low — dark lighting, fog,
  and sound design carry the horror, not custom high-fidelity models.
- **Genre tag:** Horror → Procedural Escape / Survival Horror (Roblox genre tree:
  Horror > Escape).
- **Thumbnail idea:** Tight first-person shot down a narrow, flooded concrete
  corridor that visibly warps/bends mid-frame (as if the walls are melting/shifting),
  one flickering fluorescent light, two faint glowing eyes barely visible in the dark
  at the far end. Bold dripping-text title "UNDERSINK" top, small "NO TWO RUNS ALIKE"
  tag bottom corner.

### 2. The Sequence: Puzzle Escape · score 7.9

- **Hook:** Every door is a tiny logic riddle — solve it, move on, never grind again.
- **Core loop:** Spawn into a procedurally generated micro-level that's both a logic
  puzzle and a precision-movement challenge (switches, timing jumps, pattern locks,
  moving platforms). Solve the riddle, reach the exit door in under ~2 minutes,
  completion auto-saves to DataStore, and you're dropped straight into the next
  self-contained chapter. No backtracking, no long grind maps — a tight "see puzzle →
  think → execute → escape → next" rhythm. Occasional harder "vault" chapters and a
  visible chapter-counter/streak give long-term pull without ever demanding a long
  single level.
- **Why it trends:** This week's loudest Reddit player-complaint cluster is Pain &
  Anger over "badge grinds requiring long, repetitive levels" (Tired=10, Hate=9,
  Annoying=8, Frustrating=6, Worst=4) — The Sequence is engineered as the direct
  antidote. Puzzle is also rated the least saturated genre tracked (Low competition,
  0.68 opportunity score), so there's real search volume with few polished
  competitors. Procedural generation + endless short chapters naturally produces the
  shareable "one more puzzle" loop without relying on virality or clone-chasing.
- **Solo build scope:** Reuse the existing 500-level maze obby engine almost wholesale
  — swap its procedural corridor generator for a procedural puzzle-chunk generator
  stitching together a small library of modular puzzle pieces (switch-gated doors,
  timed moving platforms, color/pattern-matching panels, sequence-memory locks). MVP:
  6–10 hand-built puzzle-piece prefabs combined via weighted random selection (no full
  constraint solver needed); per-player DataStore record `{chaptersCompleted,
  currentSeed, bestTimes}` reusing the maze game's existing save/load plumbing; single
  server, no team/PvP/voice systems; simple UI (chapter counter, timer, "Next Chapter"
  button); one reusable lobby/hub room. Content variety comes from recombination, not
  hand-authored level count, so launch is days away, with more piece types added
  post-launch.
- **Genre tag:** Puzzle / Precision Escape-Room Platformer (Obby).
- **Thumbnail idea:** A character mid-jump frozen between two glowing puzzle panels
  inside a sleek, neon-lit half-open vault door — one side shows a lock/switch puzzle
  UI overlay, the other a precision platforming gap — bold white-outlined text "ESCAPE
  THE SEQUENCE" and a countdown-timer graphic in the corner to signal both "puzzle" and
  "fast-paced" at a glance.

### 3. Heist Hollow: Steal & Stash Simulator · score 7.5

- **Hook:** Sneak your raccoon crew through never-the-same-twice neighborhoods, steal
  the loot, bank it at the Hideout, and build an idle-income empire — no feeds, no
  brainrot, just pure heist loop.
- **Core loop:** Spawn into a procedurally-generated neighborhood (new layout every
  heist) → sneak/break into houses, holding an interact-and-hold "steal" prompt on loot
  nodes (cash, jewelry, rare safes) while a rising "Heat" meter ticks — get spotted and
  Heat maxes out, you're booted from that run → escape back to your Hideout van before
  Heat caps, or risk losing the loot → bank loot at the Hideout to convert it into cash
  → spend cash on upgrades (steal speed, loot capacity, stealth, Idle Income
  generators that keep paying out even while offline) → a capped number of "jobs" per
  session gives a natural stopping point (no infinite treadmill), then a new
  neighborhood regenerates with higher-value loot tiers unlocked.
- **Why it trends:** The steal/hatch/collector loop remains the platform's single
  biggest demand driver (Steal an Egg ~1.1M CCU/300M+ plays, Steal a Brainrot's
  all-time 25.8M CCU record, Grow a Garden's 22.3M peak), and that demand provably
  survived moderation — a copycat is still in Rotrends' Oct 14 top-10. Crucially,
  Roblox only banned the *continuous feed* treadmill mechanic, not the underlying
  steal-and-collect gameplay, so Heist Hollow keeps the compliant core loop intact
  while sidestepping both of the genre's current liabilities: Steal a Brainrot's
  owners are suing 4+ copycat studios over brainrot-IP cloning (zero exposure here —
  original raccoon-heist theme), and this week's Reddit threads explicitly call the
  genre "repetitive" and "cringe" while asking for "more originality."
- **Solo build scope:** Repurpose existing proc-gen code to lay out house plots + loot
  nodes on a grid, 3–5 house tiers (shack, suburb house, mansion) with randomized loot
  tables, single server/place. Simple per-player state machine: Idle → Stealing
  (hold-to-steal + Heat accumulation) → Escaping → Banked, server-authoritative, with
  static trigger-zone Heat (no NPC AI pathing needed for v1). Hideout hub: one baseplate
  room with a Bank terminal + Upgrade shop via ProximityPrompts + GUI. Idle income via
  a simple timestamp-diff accrual calculation on join. DataStore: cash, owned upgrades,
  lifetime loot stats — a flat table. Session cap via a server-side job counter +
  cooldown. Skip for v1: PvP/robbing other players, cosmetics/trading, voice chat,
  mobile-specific UI polish.
- **Genre tag:** Simulator — Heist/Steal Collector Sim (Idle-Income subgenre).
- **Thumbnail idea:** A masked raccoon mid-leap bursting out of a suburban house
  window at night, clutching a glowing loot sack overflowing with cash and gems, a
  second raccoon driving a getaway van below; soft blue moonlit neighborhood
  background, bold yellow "HEIST HOLLOW" title text with a flashlight-beam/alarm-glow
  effect for urgency.

### 4. Shaftbound: Dig Simulator · score 7.2

- **Hook:** The mining simulator where YOU aim and swing the pick — no more auto-dig
  AFK walking.
- **Core loop:** Descend into a procedurally generated shaft → aim-and-dig: actively
  target ore veins and rock faces with timed/aimed hits (not passive auto-dig) to break
  through walls and expose loot → ore and gems auto-collect into a clean, counter-based
  inventory (numbers ticking up, no cluttered item grid) → surface to sell ore, buy a
  stronger pickaxe/drill and cosmetic upgrades → descend further into harder-to-read,
  harder-to-dig shafts for rarer ore tiers → prestige/"new vein" reset at max depth for
  a permanent multiplier, DataStore-saved, without resetting the active skill loop.
- **Why it trends:** This week's r/RobloxGameDev "Looking For Devs" flair peaked at 31
  posts around solo-built digging games, proving real builder momentum — while
  DevForum feedback on multiple live mining/digging incrementals this exact week
  repeatedly names the same five unsolved complaints: the loop gets repetitive fast, no
  skill-based interaction (auto-dig while walking), confusing controls with no
  on-screen key guide, cluttered inventory UI instead of a simple counter, and heavy
  lag when digging. Shaftbound is built to directly answer each complaint — a rare
  "fix the obvious bugs everyone is complaining about" opportunity rather than a chase
  for a new hook.
- **Solo build scope:** Procedural shaft generator (reuse existing skill): chunked
  tile/voxel layout, depth-scaled difficulty, a handful of biome palettes (dirt, stone,
  crystal cave, lava-adjacent) swapped by depth tier. Aim-and-dig mechanic: a simple
  raycast/hitbox + timing/accuracy check on click/tap (mobile-friendly single input),
  3–5 tool tiers changing swing speed/break radius — the one net-new system worth
  building well. Counter-based inventory: no physical item objects, just numeric
  counters in one clean UI panel. Persistent on-screen control-prompt HUD (2–3 states)
  fixes the #1 cited UX complaint for near-zero build cost. Static, non-procedural shop
  + upgrade stations at the surface hub. DataStore progression save (ore counts, owned
  tools, depth unlocked, prestige count) reusing existing skill. Performance pass:
  pool/stream shaft chunks instead of keeping the whole mine loaded, directly
  addressing the "heavy lag" complaint. Cut from v1: trading, PvP, pets/companions,
  leaderboards beyond a simple top-ore stat.
- **Genre tag:** Simulator > Mining/Digging Incremental (Skill-Based Interaction).
- **Thumbnail idea:** Dark, moody mine shaft shot from behind the player's shoulder: a
  glowing aim reticle locked onto a bright crystal-blue ore vein embedded in rock,
  pickaxe mid-swing with a motion-blur arc, scattered glowing ore chunks on the ground.
  Bold chunky text top-left "DIG SIMULATOR" with a smaller "AIM & SWING" tag,
  high-contrast yellow/cyan-on-black palette to pop in the Roblox discovery grid.
